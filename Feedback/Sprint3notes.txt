  Building the iMessage Layer
                                                                        
  This is the most important infrastructure decision you'll make.
  iMessage is not just a communication channel — it's where the         
  zero-friction promise lives. No app download, no sign-up, no credit
  card. Just a number someone texts. That only works if the number is an
   iMessage number.
                       
  Why iMessage specifically                                             
   
  iMessage is the default messaging app for every iPhone user in the US.
   85% of US smartphone users are on iPhone. Texting a number in
  iMessage feels like texting a friend — not using an app, not filling  
  out a form. That psychological difference is the entire product
  experience. Telegram requires a download. WhatsApp requires setup.
  iMessage requires nothing.

  How Poke.com built it — research this

  Poke.com launched in March 2026 and is doing exactly what you want to 
  do — delivering an AI assistant through iMessage. They raised $15M and
   their tech stack solves the exact same problem. Before building from 
  scratch, study how they did it. Their approach to iMessage delivery,
  session management, and identity via phone number is directly
  relevant. The founders have done interviews — find them. What they
  built in iMessage infrastructure could save you months.

  The technical path to iMessage                                        
   
  Apple does not have an official API for iMessage. This is the core    
  challenge. There are three real approaches:
                                                                        
  Option 1 — Apple Business Chat (now Apple Messages for Business)      
  Apple has a business messaging API that allows companies to send and
  receive iMessages at scale. This is the legitimate, scalable,         
  Apple-approved path. It requires applying to Apple's program, getting
  approved, and integrating via an approved messaging service partner   
  (like Salesforce, Zendesk, or a dedicated provider). Approval takes
  weeks to months and requires a legitimate business presence. This is
  the right long-term path.

  Option 2 — Mac Mini / macOS Server with jailbreak-adjacent tools      
  Some developers run a Mac Mini server with tools like BlueBubbles or a
   custom AppleScript setup that routes iMessages through a real Apple  
  ID to an API. This works but it's fragile — Apple can break it with
  any OS update, it violates Apple's terms of service, and it doesn't   
  scale beyond a handful of users. Good for prototyping, bad for
  production.          

  Option 3 — SMS with iMessage fallback
  Use a real phone number via Twilio or similar. When a client texts the
   number from an iPhone, it sends as SMS — not iMessage. It works in   
  the native Messages app but shows as a green bubble, not blue.
  Functionally identical experience for the client, technically much    
  simpler to build, no Apple approval needed. This is likely how you get
   to market fastest while applying for Apple Business Chat in parallel.

  The recommended build path

  1. Right now: stay on Telegram for internal testing with Tanya's      
  existing clients
  2. Next 30 days: set up a Twilio number and rebuild the bot on SMS —  
  clients text a real number, it works in the native Messages app, green
   bubble but zero friction
  3. In parallel: apply for Apple Messages for Business — start the     
  process now because approval takes time                               
  4. Once approved: migrate to full iMessage (blue bubble, read
  receipts, typing indicators, audio messages)                          
                  
  Identity via phone number                                             
                  
  This solves the client identification problem at the same time. Every 
  incoming message carries a phone number. That phone number maps to a
  client profile. No name needed to identify someone. No login. No      
  account. The phone number is the account. First session, Tanya asks
  for a name once — stores it permanently tied to that number. Every
  session after that, she knows exactly who she's talking to before they
   say a word.

  What to research before building                                      
   
  - Poke.com's iMessage implementation — find their technical blog      
  posts, founder interviews, or any public documentation on how they
  handle Apple's restrictions at scale                                  
  - Apple Messages for Business requirements and application process
  - Twilio's SMS to iMessage behavior — understand exactly what the
  client experience looks like on both ends                             
  - BlueBubbles as a prototyping tool — worth understanding even if you
  don't use it in production

  The Six Things That Get You There
                                   
  1. The Follow-Up Engine — Build This First
                                                                        
  This is the entire product. Everything else is secondary until this   
  works perfectly.                                                      
                                                                        
  The moment that makes this product unlike anything else: someone texts
   on Monday about a hard conversation they've been avoiding. Wednesday,
   Tanya follows up — "hey, how did that go?" Unprompted. Remembered.   
  Human. That moment is the hook. That's what gets shared. That's what
  no competitor has.   

  How it works: after every session, extract 1-3 follow-up triggers —   
  specific commitments made, emotional moments worth revisiting, topics
  the client flagged as important. Attach timing to each (2 days, 5     
  days, 1 week). Store them. Fire them automatically. The message should
   feel like Tanya remembered, not like a system pinged.

  Do not move to anything else until this is working and clients are    
  responding to follow-ups with things like "I can't believe you
  remembered that."                                                     
                  
  2. Zero Friction First Session                                        
   
  No sign-up. No credit card. No app download. Just a number to text.   
                  
  The first session is the entire sales pitch. If Tanya is good, people 
  come back. If she's not, nothing else you build matters. The barrier
  to trying needs to be nothing — because the product sells itself the  
  moment someone experiences it. One good session is worth more than any
   ad.                 

  This means the opening voice message and the first 5 exchanges need to
   be exceptional. That's the window. Optimize them obsessively.
                                                                        
  3. The Growth Loop                                                    
                       
  Retention means satisfied clients keep coming back. That's a business.
   A growth loop means satisfied clients bring new ones. That's a
  company.                                                              
                  
  The most natural trigger: after a breakthrough moment — when Tanya    
  names a shift, celebrates a milestone, or reflects progress back — she
   ends with: "A lot of people go through exactly this. If someone in   
  your life could use this, just share my number. Their first session is
   on me."             

  That's it. Word of mouth with a built-in trigger. No referral codes,  
  no incentive programs. Just a human moment that makes sharing feel
  like a gift, not a sales tactic.                                      
                  
  4. Visible Transformation — Spirit Orbs and BecomingYou

  People need to feel their progress. Not see a dashboard. Feel it.     
   
  This is why the BecomingYou section exists. A dedicated space in the  
  app where clients revisit their transformation moments pulled directly
   from session transcripts. Not a log. Not a checklist. A living record
   of who they're becoming.
                       
  The unit of progress is the Spirit Orb. Every time something real     
  shifts in a session — a breakthrough, a new way of seeing, a moment of
   genuine clarity — the client earns an orb. Shiny, golden, glowing.   
  Like the ones from Inside Out. Tappable — opens the actual moment from
   the session.        

  Never call it progress tracking. That language activates the Not      
  Enough wound. The orbs are evidence of who they already are, not a
  reminder of how far they have to go.                                  
                  
  Simple explanation for users: "Every time something shifts in your    
  session — a new way of seeing, a moment of clarity, a real 
  breakthrough — you get a Spirit Orb. It's yours to keep. Tap it       
  anytime to go back to that moment."
                       
  5. Pricing That Feels Like Nothing

  $20-30 a month. Less than one therapy session. Less than one coaching 
  call. Less than a gym membership most people don't use. The moment the
   price comparison is that easy, the decision is easy. Free first      
  session creates the experience. Low monthly price removes the
  friction. Those two things together make the funnel almost automatic.

  6. One Famous Early User

  Not a celebrity. Just someone with an audience who talks publicly     
  about what this did for them. One authentic story told in their own
  words is worth more than any campaign. Target: someone with           
  10,000-50,000 engaged followers in personal development,
  entrepreneurship, or wellness. Give them free access. Let the product
  do the work.

  Backend Bottlenecks
                     
  Client Identification — Phone number is the true identifier. Name is
  just for warmth. If no profile exists for a number, Tanya asks for the
   name once and stores it permanently. No client ever identifies
  themselves again.                                                     
                  
  End Session in App Format — Typed "end session" doesn't translate to  
  native app UI. Needs a button or gesture. The /endsession tip in the
  closing message needs a UI-appropriate replacement. Underlying flow   
  stays the same: transcript save, profile update, closing voice
  message.
